## Why this exists, in plain language

Bunyan is released under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3** (see `LICENSE`), and is also
offered under separate **commercial terms** to those who want to use it without the AGPL's obligations.
That is the "AGPL + commercial" model, and it only works if a single party holds the rights to license
the whole codebase both ways.

If someone contributes code and keeps their copyright with no licence grant to the project, that
contribution can be shipped under the AGPL and **never** under the commercial terms. One such merge
makes the commercial offering unsellable in its current form, and the damage is permanent — the code
must then be found, removed, and reimplemented by someone who never saw it.

**Signing this agreement does not take your copyright away.** You keep it, and you remain free to use
your own contribution anywhere else, for anything, forever. What you grant is a licence letting the
project use it too, including commercially.

This is also why Bunyan uses a **CLA rather than a DCO**. A Developer Certificate of Origin certifies
that you have the right to submit the code; it does not grant the relicensing right the model above
depends on.
